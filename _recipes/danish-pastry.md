# Danish Pastry

## Ingredients

- 2 cups unsalted butter, divided
- 5 1/2 cups all-purpose flour
- 1/2 cup granulated sugar
- 4 1/2 tsp instant yeast
- 2 tsp salt
- 2 tsp vanilla extract
- 1 1/2 cups milk
- 2 eggs
- 8 oz cream cheese, room temperature
- 3 tbsp granulated sugar
- 1 egg
- 1/4 tsp salt
- 1 cup fruit jam, preserves, or canned pie filling
- 1 egg white
- 1 tbsp water
- 1 1/2 cups powdered sugar
- 2 tbsp milk

## Instructions

1. Cut 2 tbsp from the total 2 cups unsalted butter and work those 2 tbsp into 5 1/2 cups all-purpose flour, 1/2 cup granulated sugar, 4 1/2 tsp instant yeast, and 2 tsp salt.
2. Add 2 tsp vanilla extract, 1 1/2 cups milk, and 2 eggs, then mix and knead until you have a cohesive but sticky dough.
3. Let the dough rest for 10 minutes while you shape the remaining butter into 2 cold rectangles about 6 by 9 inches each.
4. Roll the dough into a 12 by 24 inch rectangle, enclose the 2 butter blocks in thirds, seal the edges, then roll and fold the dough into a packet.
5. Chill the dough for 20 minutes, then roll and fold it 2 more times, wrapping and chilling it for at least 2 hours at the end.
6. Stir 8 oz cream cheese, 3 tbsp granulated sugar, 1 egg, and 1/4 tsp salt together until smooth for the cheese filling.
7. Divide the chilled dough into 36 pieces, roll each piece into a ball, and flatten each one into a 3 to 3 1/2 inch round with a slightly raised edge.
8. Arrange the rounds on lined baking sheets, cover them lightly, and let them rise for about 1 hour while you heat the oven to 400 F.
9. Press the centers back down, spoon a heaping teaspoon of cheese filling or 1 heaping teaspoon fruit filling into each center, and brush the edges with 1 egg white whisked with 1 tbsp water.
10. Bake the pastries one sheet at a time for 15 to 18 minutes, until golden brown.
11. Whisk 1 1/2 cups powdered sugar with 2 tbsp milk into a drizzleable glaze and spoon it over the cooled pastries.

## Serve With

- Coffee, tea, or fruit.

## Notes

- This can easily be split across 2 days.
- Very cold butter matters for the lamination.
